# Shortcut learning and dataset bias in deepfake / AI-generated-image detection

Literature survey compiled 2026-09-08. Every entry below is marked with how it was
verified. "Fetched abs" = the arXiv abstract page was retrieved and the title,
author list and abstract read off it. "Crossref" = publisher metadata retrieved
from api.crossref.org (authoritative for venue/DOI/pages). "Fetched full text" =
the HTML or PDF body was retrieved; numbers taken from tables relayed by the
fetch. Anything not confirmed this way is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Theoretical frame

**Geirhos, R., Jacobsen, J.-H., Michaelis, C., Zemel, R., Brendel, W., Bethge, M.,
Wichmann, F. A. — "Shortcut learning in deep neural networks."**
*Nature Machine Intelligence* 2(11), 665-673, 10 Nov 2020.
DOI 10.1038/s42256-020-00257-z · arXiv:2004.07780
Verified: fetched abs + Crossref (venue, volume, issue, pages, date all confirmed).

Definition to quote: "Shortcuts are decision rules that perform well on standard
benchmarks but fail to transfer to more challenging testing conditions, such as
real-world scenarios." The paper is a perspective, not an empirical detection
paper; it supplies the vocabulary (shortcut, o.o.d. test, "recommendations for
model interpretation and benchmarking") that the detection papers below inherit.

## 2. The "each source corpus maps to exactly one class" confound

### 2.1 The clearest statements of the confound

**Zheng, C., Lin, C., Zhao, Z., Wang, H., Guo, X., Liu, S., Shen, C. —
"Breaking Semantic Artifacts for Generalized AI-generated Image Detection."**
NeurIPS 2024 (Advances in NeurIPS 37), main track.
Verified: fetched the NeurIPS proceedings abstract page and the official NeurIPS
slide deck (proceedings.neurips.cc/.../6dddcff5b115b40c998a08fbd1cea4d7).
**No arXiv ID confirmed.** A search result attributed arXiv:2412.09817 to this
paper; I fetched 2412.09817 and it is a *different* paper ("Enhancing Multimodal
Large Language Models Complex Reason via Similarity Computation"). Cite the
NeurIPS proceedings URL, not an arXiv ID.

This is the closest paper to the confound as stated. From the abstract: "it
remains unexplored whether these detectors can generalize across different image
scenes, e.g., images from different datasets with different semantics. In this
paper, we reveal that existing detectors suffer from substantial Accuracy drops
in such cross-scene generalization. In particular, we attribute their failures to
'semantic artifacts' in both real and generated images, to which detectors may
overfit." Slides state it more bluntly: "Real images with different semantics
exhibit different artifacts. Semantic artifacts can be inherited by generative
models," and the conclusion slide: "Existing detectors suffer from performance
drops on cross-scene images, even though the images are generated by models with
the same structure (LDM)."
Numbers (from abstract, verified): +2.08 pp absolute average cross-scene accuracy
over prior work; +10.59 pp absolute averaged over all 31 test sets (7 GANs, 18
diffusion variants, 6 CNN-based generators).
UNVERIFIED: a search snippet quoted "51.18%" for a Bedroom→Church transfer with
patch shuffle. I could not fetch the paper PDF (>10 MB fetch limit). Do not cite
that number without checking the PDF yourself.

**Guillaro, F., Zingarini, G., Usman, B., Sud, A., Cozzolino, D., Verdoliva, L. —
"A Bias-Free Training Paradigm for More General AI-generated Image Detection."**
arXiv:2412.17671 (v1 23 Dec 2024, v2 3 Apr 2025). Project page grip-unina.github.io/B-Free/
Verified: fetched abs; details and numbers from fetched HTML (v2).
Venue: UNVERIFIED — the arXiv page shows only cs.CV, no accepted-at comment.

This is the direct constructive answer to the confound. B-Free builds fakes *from*
the reals: starting from 51,517 MS-COCO real images, a Stable Diffusion 2.1
inpainting model regenerates each image with an empty mask, so "semantic
alignment between real and fake images" holds and "any differences [...] stem
solely from the subtle artifacts introduced by AI generation." 309,102 fakes in
total, plus content augmentation that inpaints objects in/out.
Bias taxonomy the paper uses (fetched full text): **format bias** (real = lossy
JPEG, fake = lossless PNG), **content bias**, **resolution bias** (resizing "washes
out subtle low-level forensic clues").
The content-bias demonstration (Fig. 2, fetched full text): the *same* detector
(RINE) gives *opposite* predictions on the *same* generator (DALL·E 3) depending
on whether it was trained with RAISE or with COCO as the real corpus. That is the
confound made visible in one figure — worth reproducing in a thesis.
Headline results (fetched full text, Table 5 / Table 7): 96.3 average balanced
accuracy across 27 generators vs 67.3 for the next best (CoDE); 90.4 overall
average across five benchmarks vs 74.2 for CoDE; on "GenImage unbiased" (5k
real/fake with matched JPEG compression) 89.3% vs 71.7%.

**Gye, S., Ko, J., Shon, H., Kwon, M., Kim, J. — "SFLD: Reducing the content bias
for AI-generated Image Detection."** WACV 2025 (Oral). arXiv:2502.17105 (24 Feb 2025).
Verified: fetched abs; venue string "IEEE/CVF WACV 2025, Oral" is the arXiv
comments field. A CVF open-access PDF URL for WACV2025 also appears in search
(openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Gye_Reducing_the_Content_Bias_...),
but note the CVF title reads "Reducing the Content Bias for AI-Generated Image
Detection" — the "SFLD:" prefix is arXiv-only.

Contributes **TwinSynths**, "a new benchmark generation methodology that constructs
visually near-identical pairs of real and synthetic images to ensure high quality
and content preservation." Same fix as B-Free, arrived at independently, and it
names the failure mode of existing benchmarks explicitly: "low image quality,
insufficient content preservation, and limited class diversity."

**Shuai, C., Fan, S., Zou, C., Gong, B., Lian, W., Bi, X., Liu, Z., Ba, Z., Ren, K. —
"When Detectors Forget Forensics: Blocking Semantic Shortcuts for Generalizable
AI-Generated Image Detection."** arXiv:2603.09242 (10 Mar 2026, rev. 3 Jun 2026).
Verified: fetched abs. Venue: none listed (cs.CV).
Names the mechanism "semantic fallback": "forensic fine-tuning fails to fully
reshape the representation space [...] the resulting representations remain
organized along high-level semantic structures rather than manipulation-specific
forensic cues." Fix is SVD-based suppression of the dominant semantic subspace of
a frozen CLIP encoder. Only number in the abstract is a 15× compute reduction for
the mini-batch SVD; detection numbers not in the abstract.

**Zhang, H., He, Q., Bi, X., Liu, B., Pun, C.-M., Xiao, B. — "Combating Pattern and
Content Bias: Adversarial Feature Learning for Generalized AI-Generated Image
Detection."** arXiv:2604.12353 (14 Apr 2026). Verified: fetched abs.
Explicitly separates "generation-pattern bias" from "content bias" and suppresses
both adversarially (MAFL). Reports +10.89% accuracy and +8.57% AP over baselines
(abstract).

**Nebioglu, E., Bilgiç, E., Popescu, A. — "AI-Generated Image Detectors Overrely on
Global Artifacts: Evidence from Inpainting Exchange."** arXiv:2602.00192 (30 Jan 2026).
Verified: fetched abs.
Different confound, same family, and methodologically the cleanest intervention
in the list. INP-X restores the original pixels *outside* an inpainted region
while keeping the synthesized content inside it. Under this intervention
"pretrained state-of-the-art detectors, including commercial ones, exhibit a
dramatic drop in accuracy (e.g., from 91% to 55%), frequently approaching chance
level." Cause identified: "VAE-based reconstruction induces a subtle but pervasive
spectral shift across the entire image, including unedited regions." 90K test set
of real / inpainted / exchanged triplets.

**Willi, M., Mathys, M., Graber, M. — "Synthetic Image Detection with CLIP:
Understanding and Assessing Predictive Cues."** arXiv:2602.12381 (12 Feb 2026,
rev. 15 Aug 2026). Verified: fetched abs.
Introduces **SynthCLIC**, "which pairs real photographs with caption-matched,
high-quality diffusion-generated counterparts" — another matched-content benchmark.
Numbers from the abstract: CLIP-based linear detectors reach 0.96 mAP on a
GAN-heavy benchmark, 0.92 on SynthCLIC, but cross-family transfer to CNNSpot falls
to **0.42 mAP**. The interpretability finding is the useful one for a bias thesis:
"higher synthetic scores correspond to cleaner, more compositionally controlled,
and technically polished images, whereas lower scores correspond to messier capture
conditions and provenance cues characteristic of real photographs." That is corpus
identity, described in words.

### 2.2 Where the confound comes from — the canonical datasets

**Wang, S.-Y., Wang, O., Zhang, R., Owens, A., Efros, A. A. — "CNN-generated images
are surprisingly easy to spot... for now."** CVPR 2020. arXiv:1912.11035.
Verified: fetched abs (comments field reads "Accepted to CVPR 2020"); dataset
layout confirmed from the official CNNDetection GitHub README (fetched).
ForenSynths: training set is 20 LSUN object categories, `0_real` from LSUN and
`1_fake` from ProGAN models trained per category; test set covers 13 generators
(ProGAN, StyleGAN, StyleGAN2, BigGAN, CycleGAN, StarGAN, GauGAN, CRN, IMLE, SITD,
SAN, DeepFake, whichfaceisreal). The README does not state per-generator image
counts or a pairing rule. UNVERIFIED (search snippet only): the frequently quoted
"18,000 fake images per category". Note that in ForenSynths the *training* pair is
comparatively benign — ProGAN was trained on LSUN, so real and fake share the
category — but each *test* generator brings its own real corpus (ImageNet for
BigGAN, CelebA for StarGAN, COCO for GauGAN, …), which is exactly where corpus
identity becomes a per-row confound in the famous cross-generator table.

**Zhu, M., Chen, H., Yan, Q., Huang, X., Lin, G., Li, W., Tu, Z., Hu, H., Hu, J.,
Wang, Y. — "GenImage: A Million-Scale Benchmark for Detecting AI-Generated Image."**
arXiv:2306.08571. Verified: fetched abs. NeurIPS 2023 D&B track: UNVERIFIED (the
arXiv page shows only a GitHub link in comments).
Defines the two tasks everyone now reports: "The cross-generator image classification
task measures the performance of a detector trained on one generator when tested on
the others. The degraded image classification task assesses the capability of the
detectors in handling degraded images such as low-resolution, blurred, and
compressed images." GenImage is also the dataset that Grommelt et al. take apart
(§3.1) — real = ImageNet JPEGs, fake = generator PNGs.

**Rössler, A., Cozzolino, D., Verdoliva, L., Riess, C., Thies, J., Nießner, M. —
"FaceForensics++: Learning to Detect Manipulated Facial Images."**
ICCV 2019, pp. 1-11. DOI 10.1109/ICCV.2019.00009 · arXiv:1901.08971.
Verified: fetched abs + Crossref (venue, year, pages, DOI).
Relevant here mainly for its protocol: the benchmark is built "at random compression
level and size", with a hidden test set, and the source (pristine) and manipulated
videos come from the *same* 1,000 source videos — i.e. FF++ does *not* have the
corpus confound, because real and fake share the base footage. That makes it a
useful contrast case against the image-generation benchmarks. Its own well-known
shortcut is compression level (c0/c23/c40), see §3.4.

**Bammey, Q. — "Synthbuster: Towards Detection of Diffusion Model Generated Images."**
*IEEE Open Journal of Signal Processing*, vol. 5, pp. 1-9, 2024.
DOI 10.1109/OJSP.2023.3337714. Verified: Crossref.
Worth flagging as an example of the confound rather than a fix: real images are
RAISE raw photographs, fakes come from nine text-to-image generators. Real and fake
share neither content nor capture pipeline. B-Free uses it as a *test* set (1k real
/ 9k fake in raw format, chosen precisely to avoid compression artefacts), which is
the defensible way to use it.

### 2.3 The identity/demographic bias line (adjacent, not the same confound)

**Dong, S., Wang, J., Ji, R., Liang, J., Fan, H., Ge, Z. — "Implicit Identity Leakage:
The Stumbling Block to Improving Deepfake Detection Generalization."** CVPR 2023.
arXiv:2210.14457. Verified: fetched abs (comments: "Accepted by CVPR 2023").
Face-video analogue of the confound: the detector learns *who is in the video*
rather than the manipulation, because in FF++-style datasets identity correlates
with label at the video level.

**Lin, L., He, X., Ju, Y., Wang, X., Ding, F., Hu, S. — "Preserving Fairness
Generalization in Deepfake Detection."** CVPR 2024. arXiv:2402.17229 (27 Feb 2024).
Verified: fetched abs (comments confirm CVPR 2024). CVF PDF also indexed.
This is demographic fairness (race, gender disparities), not corpus identity. Cite
it if the thesis needs a fairness section; it is not evidence for the source-corpus
confound.

**Wang, Y., Yang, Y., Liu, J., Zhu, F., Wu, L., Chua, T.-S. — "Suppressing
Forgery-Specific Shortcuts for Generalizable Deepfake Detection."**
arXiv:2606.01843 (1 Jun 2026). Verified: fetched abs.
Explicitly frames the problem as shortcuts: "naive implementations could allow
models to rely on trivial correlations, such as remembering identity or background
from real images." Method S³ isolates a shortcut subspace with a linear probe + SVD
and suppresses it. The *linear-probe-for-shortcut-subspace* idea is a transferable
audit tool.

## 3. Compression, resolution, resampling as shortcuts

### 3.1 The headline result

**Grommelt, P., Weiss, L., Pfreundt, F.-J., Keuper, J. — "Fake or JPEG? Revealing
Common Biases in Generated Image Detection Datasets."**
ECCV 2024 Workshops, LNCS vol. 15644, pp. 80-95, Springer, 2025.
DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-92089-9_6 · arXiv:2403.17608 (v1 26 Mar 2024, v2 28 Mar 2024).
Verified: fetched abs + Crossref (container title, year, pages, publisher, DOI);
numbers below from the fetched arXiv HTML v2.

**Title correction for your bibliography:** both the arXiv page and the Springer
Crossref record give the title as "...Generated Image Detection Datasets". There is
no "**and Detectors**" in the published title. The brief you were given has it wrong.

The bias, concretely:
- *JPEG bias.* GenImage reals are ImageNet JPEGs, "the majority of images compressed
  using a quality factor of 96"; GenImage fakes are stored uncompressed (PNG).
  Compression status alone therefore carries the label.
- *Size bias.* Fakes have one fixed size per generator — 1024×1024 Midjourney,
  512×512 SD 1.4/1.5 and Wukong, 256×256 GLIDE/ADM/VQDM, 128×128 BigGAN — while
  ImageNet reals have a diverse size distribution. Detectors do worse on real images
  whose dimensions happen to match the generator's.
- Debiasing recipe: keep only reals compressed at QF 96 and apply matching JPEG-96
  to the fakes; for the size control restrict to 512×512 generators, keep only reals
  in [450, 550] px (which cuts the training pool from ~320k to 75k), centre-crop to
  450 then resize to 224.
- Result (Table 4): mean cross-generator accuracy rises **71.68% → 82.74%** for
  ResNet50 (+11.06 pp) and **74.09% → 85.83%** for Swin-T (+11.74 pp).
- Robustness (Table 3): +13.26 accuracy points under JPEG-95, +8.75 under JPEG-80,
  +4.49 under JPEG-60.
- The paper does *not* report a classifier trained on JPEG/size metadata alone. If
  your thesis wants that experiment, it is an open slot — the closest published
  analogue is the trivial-baseline probe in VidAudit (§4.4).

### 3.2 The GAN-era evidence that resizing/recompression destroys the signal

**Gragnaniello, D., Cozzolino, D., Marra, F., Poggi, G., Verdoliva, L. — "Are GAN
generated images easy to detect? A critical analysis of the state-of-the-art."**
IEEE ICME 2021. arXiv:2104.02617 (6 Apr 2021).
Verified: fetched abs *and* extracted the full PDF text, so the numbers below are
read directly off the paper.

Protocol discipline worth copying:
- "In our experimental analysis all networks are trained and tested on the very same
  data" — a matched harness, decided in 2021.
- Table 2 records, per detector, the *test strategy from the original paper* (no
  crop/no resize vs central crop 224 vs resize 128 vs resize 299), because that
  preprocessing choice is itself a confound.
- They deliberately exclude CelebA-HQ reals: "as done elsewhere, since they are
  GAN-upsampled versions of the low-resolution real images." That is an explicit
  refusal of a source-corpus artefact, and a good sentence to quote.
- They report AUC, accuracy@0.5, and Pd@5%FAR and Pd@1%FAR, arguing that for
  large-scale screening "only very low FARs are acceptable." AUC-only reporting hides
  the failure: "AUC results on low-resolution images are generally very good [...]
  several methods exceeding the 0.9 level. However, accuracy results are much less
  encouraging [...] Considering the Pd@FAR metric, results become pretty bad."

Numbers (Table 3, accuracy / Pd@5%, ProGAN-trained Wang2020 baseline vs the best
"no-down" variant vs the same variant trained on StyleGAN2):

| StyleGAN2        | 71.5 / 69.0  | 92.2 / 88.8  | 99.9 / 100.0 |
| BigGAN           | 59.2 / 45.2  | 93.5 / 92.0  | 96.5 / 99.4  |

Text findings: avoiding down-sampling in the first block gives "an average gain of

### 3.3 Diffusion-era version of the same result

**Corvi, R., Cozzolino, D., Zingarini, G., Poggi, G., Nagano, K., Verdoliva, L. —
"On the detection of synthetic images generated by diffusion models."**
ICASSP 2023, pp. 1-5. DOI 10.1109/ICASSP49357.2023.10095167 · arXiv:2211.00680.
Verified: fetched abs + Crossref (venue, pages, DOI); numbers below from the fetched
ar5iv full text.
Their "social network" degradation: "a crop with random (large) size and position is
selected, resized to 200×200 pixels, and compressed using a random JPEG quality
factor from 65 to 100."
Effect on DALL·E 2 test images: Grag2021 goes from 51.9% acc / 94.9% AUC (clean PNG)
to 50.0% / **64.4%** AUC; Wang2020 goes from 50.3% / 85.8% to 50.0% / **44.8%** —
i.e. below chance. Paper's explanation: "forensics traces are very weak and can be
easily removed."
Test-set construction: reals from COCO, ImageNet and UCID; fakes from 12 generators;
COCO captions used as prompts for the text-to-image models. Note the mixed situation
— prompts are COCO-derived so content is loosely matched, but the real and fake
*pixels* still come from separate corpora with separate capture/coding pipelines.
Treat any claim that they fully controlled content as UNVERIFIED.

**Corvi, R., Cozzolino, D., Poggi, G., Nagano, K., Verdoliva, L. — "Intriguing
properties of synthetic images: from generative adversarial networks to diffusion
models."** CVPR 2023 Workshops (WMF), pp. 973-982.
DOI 10.1109/CVPRW59228.2023.00104 · arXiv:2304.06408.
Verified: fetched abs + Crossref (venue, pages, DOI). The CVF open-access HTML page
returned 403 to my fetch, but the CVF URL exists:
openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/WMF/html/Corvi_Intriguing_Properties_...
Finding (2) in the abstract is the one for a bias thesis: "when the dataset used to
train the model lacks sufficient variety, its biases can be transferred to the
generated images." That is the mechanism by which the *training corpus of the
generator* leaks into the fake class. Findings (1) and (3) are the spectral/
autocorrelation artefacts and the mid-high-frequency energy difference — i.e. the
signal that compression and resampling destroy.

### 3.4 Compression as a shortcut, 2026

**Aliyev, A., Rustamov, S. — "Data Diversity, Not Frequency Invariance: A Controlled
and Self-Audited Study of Compression-Robust Deepfake Detection."**
arXiv:2608.28685 (26 Aug 2026). 38 pp., "Submitted to IEEE Access" (so: preprint,
not peer reviewed). Verified: fetched abs.
Methodologically the most disciplined paper in this whole list and directly usable
as a template: **pre-registered protocol**, capacity- and augmentation-matched
controls, multi-seed, and a published **self-audit** in which the authors found four
defects in their own negative result and pre-specified re-tests. Findings: a plain
EfficientNet-B0 trained on multi-quality data beat the frequency-invariance
architecture (CAFRL) at every compression level on the FF++ test split, by 3.66 AUC
points at CRF 40; the repaired baseline recipe recovered 3.96 points; and robustness
under H.264 re-encoding "came instead from data diversity: real constant-rate-factor
variants beat synthetic JPEG augmentation by 7.3 points." Caveat stated by the
authors: "The evidence is FaceForensics++-family, GAN-era and single-codec."

**Sundara Rajan, A., Lee, Y. J. — "Stay-Positive: A Case for Ignoring Real Image
Features in Fake Image Detection."** arXiv:2502.07778 (11 Feb 2025, rev. 26 May 2025).
Verified: fetched abs. Venue: none listed.
Argues the shortcut is on the *real* side: "detectors tendency to rely on spurious
patterns, such as compression artifacts [...] These issues often stem from specific
patterns that the detector associates with the real data distribution." Their rule —
"an image should be classified as fake if and only if it contains artifacts
introduced by the generative model" — is a clean normative statement to quote when
you justify a matched-source protocol.

**Ricker, J., Fischer, A., Quiring, E. — "BIAS-ID: A Framework for Analyzing
Transformation Biases in AI-Generated Image Detectors."**
arXiv:2605.31153 (29 May 2026). Verified: fetched abs; numbers from fetched HTML v1.
This is the audit instrument for §3 and belongs in a methods chapter. Six detectors
(UnivFD, DRCT, RINE, AIDE, SPAI, B-Free) × two datasets (Synthbuster; SynthCLIC) ×
five transformations (JPEG, WebP, resize, rotation, grayscale). Two metrics: **score
shift** ΔT_l (transformed-image score minus original-image score, in [-1, 1]) and
**Aggregated Transform Sensitivity (ATS)** averaged over transformation levels.
Selected ATS values (real / fake): JPEG — AIDE -0.547 / -0.528, SPAI 0.099 / -0.222,
B-Free 0.045 / -0.093 (least biased). WebP — DRCT +0.549 on reals (pushed toward
"fake"), RINE 0.009 / -0.401. Rotation ±90° — UnivFD 0.348 / 0.287. Grayscale —
SPAI -0.124 / -0.863. Conclusion: "several state-of-the-art detection methods are
strongly affected by biases" while scoring well under standard conditions.

## 4. Evaluation protocols

### 4.1 Cross-generator / train-on-one-test-on-N

- **ForenSynths / CNNDetection protocol** (Wang et al., CVPR 2020, arXiv:1912.11035):
  train on ProGAN + LSUN only, test on 13 generators. This is the protocol every
  later table copies. Its weakness is §2.2: each test row swaps in a different real
  corpus.
- **GenImage cross-generator protocol** (Zhu et al., arXiv:2306.08571): train on one
  of eight generators, test on the other seven; plus a degraded-image task
  (low-res, blur, compression). Verified by fetching the abs.
- **DF40** (Yan, Z., Yao, T., Chen, S., Zhao, Y., Fu, X., Zhu, J., Luo, D., Wang, C.,
  Ding, S., Wu, Y., Yuan, L. — "DF40: Toward Next-Generation Deepfake Detection",
  NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks Track, arXiv:2406.13495). Verified: fetched
  arXiv abs *and* the NeurIPS proceedings abstract page (venue + track confirmed).
  40 forgery techniques, "4 standard evaluation protocols", 8 detectors, >2,000
  evaluations, "7 new insightful findings". **The four protocols are named only in
  the body, not the abstract — UNVERIFIED, you will need the PDF.** The motivation
  quoted on the proceedings page is the relevant one: the field's "common practice:
  training detectors on one specific dataset (e.g., FF++) and testing them on other
  prevalent deepfake datasets" is itself the thing under criticism.
- **Leave-one-generator-out (LOGO).** Used by name in VidAudit (§4.4). A generic
  definition appears across several arXiv papers ("a single generative model is used
  for training and validation in each iteration, and the remaining models are used
  for evaluation"), but I could not attribute the *coinage* to a single originating
  paper. Treat "who defines LOGO" as UNVERIFIED; cite VidAudit for a worked,
  audited LOGO table and ForenSynths for the ancestor of the design.

### 4.3 Protocols that hold JPEG / resolution constant across classes

- **Grommelt et al. debiased GenImage** (§3.1): reals restricted to QF 96, fakes
  JPEG-96 encoded to match; size window [450, 550] px for 512×512 generators.
- **"GenImage unbiased"** as used by B-Free: 5k real / 5k fake "with matched JPEG
  compression" (fetched from B-Free full text).
- **Gragnaniello et al.**: per-detector preprocessing recorded and held fixed;
  GAN-upsampled reals excluded.
- **VidAudit control C1** (§4.4): "every input passes through one H.264 pipeline
  before feature extraction" — the video analogue, and the cleanest formulation of
  "re-encode everything through one canonical codec so format cannot carry label".
- **Zhou & Wang** (§4.4) show what happens without it: "without unified re-encoding,
  AUROC under JPEG-50 *exceeds* the clean condition."

### 4.4 Controlled / audit protocol papers, 2025-2026

**Cakiroglu, M. O., Lu, Z., Dalkilic, M., Kurban, H. — "Auditing Generalization in
AI-Generated Video Detection: A Six-Control Protocol and the VidAudit Toolkit."**
arXiv:2606.31004 (30 Jun 2026). github.com/KurbanIntelligenceLab/vidaudit
Verified: fetched abs *and* HTML v1 (control definitions and numbers below are quoted
from the body).
**This is the single most useful paper for your protocol chapter**, even though it is
video. It contains the existence proof the brief was looking for: "a three-feature
clip-length classifier reaches a leave-one-generator-out (LOGO) AUC of 0.998 on
GenVidBench under unaudited evaluation, while measuring nothing about motion." And
the survey result: of 20 papers, **none** applied all six controls.

The six controls, as defined in the paper:
1. **C1 Canonical re-encode** — "every input passes through one H.264 pipeline before
   feature extraction."
2. **C2 Leakage-audited K-non-I-frame filter** — "a trivial-baseline classifier (here,
   three frame-count features) is fit on the uncontrolled pipeline to measure an
   upper bound on leakage."
3. **C3 Real-vs-real coherence probe** — "a classifier is trained to discriminate the
   two real sources, giving a measurable floor on dataset-bias contribution."
4. **C4 Matched-harness comparison** — "every compared detector is re-fit under the
   same readout (an L2-regularized logistic regression)."
5. **C5 Multi-seed stability and bootstrap CIs** — "every principal number is reported
   with its multi-seed stability and a bootstrap confidence interval."
6. **C6 Cross-dataset validation** — re-run the whole audit on a benchmark "whose
   generator roster, real-clip pool, and recording pipeline were curated separately."

C3 is the direct instrument for the corpus-identity confound: train a classifier to
tell the two *real* corpora apart, and treat its AUC as the floor any real-vs-fake
number must clear. Reported numbers: trivial clip-length classifier 0.998 → **0.529**
under audit; CLIP baseline LOGO-OOD 0.852 against a real-vs-real floor of 0.766, i.e.
a margin of only +0.086 — "a CLIP baseline is caught carrying dataset identity";
WaveRep 0.996 against a real-vs-real floor of 0.534, margin +0.462. And at a
deployable FPR of 0.1%, WaveRep recall 0.82, ReStraV 0.63, while FVMD / RAFT /
TemporalSpec collapse to ≤0.03 despite high AUC — "the leaderboard order changes."
Their recommendation: report an audited tuple (AUC, above-floor margin,
operating-point recall, calibration) instead of one number.

**Zhou, J., Wang, M. — "How Fragile Are Training-Free AI-Generated Image Detectors?
A Controlled Audit of Score Direction, Preprocessing, and Compression."**
arXiv:2606.20488 (18 Jun 2026). Verified: fetched abs (all numbers below are in the
abstract, so they are directly verified).
Image-domain controlled audit on a 1,500-image GenImage-derived benchmark, seven
generators, JPEG QF 70 and 50. Three findings:
(i) "Implementation details masquerade as method differences": swapping the LPIPS
backbone AlexNet→VGG-16 changes overall AUROC by **+0.085**; switching between
resize-to-512 and native-resolution preprocessing "flips per-generator conclusions by
up to **0.38 AUROC**."
(ii) Score direction is a hyperparameter, not a property: the RIGID-style score is
*inverted* (AUROC < 0.5) on SD1.5 and Wukong at σ=0.05, recovers above 0.5 for every
generator at σ=0.01, and collapses to **0.15** at σ=0.3.
(iii) "Dataset format bias inflates robustness claims: without unified re-encoding,
AUROC under JPEG-50 exceeds the clean condition for the AlexNet-backbone
reconstruction score; after bias correction the residual anomaly localizes to a
single generator (BigGAN)."

**Michels, J., Jorissen, L., Michiels, N. — "Dataset Biases and Shortcut Learning in
Motion-Based AI-Generated Video Detection."** arXiv:2607.00948 (1 Jul 2026).
Verified: fetched abs.
Video, but the cleanest published demonstration of the general pattern: four
motion-based detectors, "significant preprocessing and sampling biases [...] account
for a substantial portion of their reported performance"; the shortcut is that "AI-
generated videos generally exhibit less inter-frame movement than real videos"; and
"performance collapses to near-random levels when evaluated on a dataset that does
not contain this motion bias." Dataset rebalancing plus simple spatial augmentation
degrades all four. Closing line is quotable: "more representative, unbiased datasets
and more robust evaluation protocols."

**Pellegrini, L., Cozzolino, D., Pandolfini, S., Maltoni, D., Ferrara, M.,
Verdoliva, L., Prati, M., Ramilli, M. — "AI-GenBench: A New Ongoing Benchmark for
AI-Generated Image Detection."** Verimedia workshop, IJCNN 2025. arXiv:2504.20865
(29 Apr 2025). Verified: fetched abs (venue is the arXiv comments field).
Contributes a *temporal* protocol: detectors are incrementally trained on generators
in historical order, testing transfer forward in time (GAN → diffusion). Explicitly
motivated by protocol defects — "overcomes key limitations of current approaches,
including arbitrary dataset splits, unfair comparisons, and excessive computational
demands", with "clear evaluation rules and controlled augmentation strategies."

**Gushchin, A. et al. (54 authors) — "NTIRE 2026 Challenge on Robust AI-Generated
Image Detection in the Wild."** CVPR 2026 NTIRE Workshop. arXiv:2604.11487
(13 Apr 2026). Verified: fetched abs.
108,750 real and 185,750 generated images, 42 generators, **36 image transformations**
(crop, resize, compress, blur); scored by ROC AUC over transformed *and* untransformed
images together; 511 registrants, 20 valid submissions. Useful as the current
community consensus on "robustness must be inside the metric, not an appendix."

**Li, C., Wang, X., Li, M., Miao, B., Sun, P., Zhang, Y., Ji, X., Zhu, Y. —
"Bridging the Gap Between Ideal and Real-world Evaluation: Benchmarking AI-Generated
Image Detection in Challenging Scenarios."** ICCV 2025. arXiv:2509.09172 (11 Sep 2025).
Verified: fetched abs (venue in comments).
RRDataset, three axes: scenario generalization (seven content domains — a content-bias
control), internet-transmission robustness (repeated social-media re-sharing), and
re-digitization robustness (four screen/print recapture methods). 17 detectors, 10
VLMs, 192 human participants.

**Ren, S., Zhou, Y., Shen, X., Zewde, K., Duong, T., Huang, G., Tiangratanakul, H.,
Ng, T., Wei, E., Xue, J. — "How well are open sourced AI-generated image detection
models out-of-the-box: A comprehensive benchmark study."** arXiv:2602.07814
(8 Feb 2026). Verified: fetched abs.
Zero-shot evaluation of 16 methods / 23 detector variants over 12 datasets, 2.6M
images, 291 generators. Numbers relevant to protocol fragility: detector rankings are
unstable across dataset pairs (Spearman ρ 0.01-0.87); a 37 pp gap between best (75.0%
mean accuracy) and worst (37.5%); **"training data alignment critically impacts
generalization, causing up to 20-60% performance variance within architecturally
identical detector families"** — i.e. the corpus you trained on, not the architecture,
decides the result; modern commercial generators (Flux Dev, Firefly v4, Midjourney v7)
score 18-30%. Friedman χ²=121.01, p<10⁻¹⁶, Kendall W=0.524.

## 5. Gaps worth knowing about

1. **No published image-domain paper trains a classifier on JPEG/size metadata alone**
   as a leakage probe. Grommelt et al. document the bias but do not run that probe;
   VidAudit runs the video analogue (C2). This is a genuinely open, cheap experiment.
2. **No image-domain "real-vs-real coherence probe" paper.** VidAudit's C3 is video.
   Training a classifier to separate two *real* corpora (e.g. COCO vs LSUN vs RAISE)
   and publishing that AUC as the floor for a detection benchmark appears unclaimed
   in the image literature.
3. **"Leave-one-generator-out" has no traceable originating citation.** Cite a paper
   that reports the table (VidAudit, or GenImage's cross-generator task) rather than
   claiming an originator.
4. **The brief's title "Fake or JPEG? ... Datasets and Detectors" is wrong** — see §3.1.
5. **The brief's "The Adverse Effect of Compression"**: I could not find any paper by
   that or a closely similar title. UNVERIFIED / probably does not exist under that
   name. The nearest real items are Gragnaniello et al. (§3.2), Corvi et al. (§3.3)
   and Aliyev & Rustamov (§3.4).
